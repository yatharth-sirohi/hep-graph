Input: `docs/atlas-worklog/QUOTE_CHECK_TRIAGE_INPUT.json` (134 items from `scripts/atlas_quote_check.py`).
Sources were fetched and read on 2026-10-26 to 2026-10-28; the verifier did not write any of the records.
`inspirehep.net` was never contacted, no encyclopaedia is cited by any record this work added, and the
`cds.cern.ch` bot check was not bypassed.

Verdicts (an item may carry two): checker miss with the record repointed 39;
checker miss that the re-check verifies once the network was back, nothing wrong in the record (the original fetch had failed) 16;
checker miss verified by hand 17;

corrected quote 9; replaced source 12; removed 1; note added 37 (counting the derivation notes some forks added to quotes that already had one).
One claim was removed ("led by MPIK Heidelberg" in the GALLEX/GNO record, item 10, which no source read supports); every other unsupported value was re-sourced rather than dropped.
76 records and 11 source records changed; validator ends at 0 errors; `pytest -k atlas` passes; the build runs
(`static/atlas/generated` was not committed).

34 of the 134 items carry a finding beyond the checker's failure: 23 where a value, date, claim or note was wrong or unsupported,
and 11 where a locator was wrong, a bound had no quote of its own, or a qualifier was missing (summary at the end).
The other 100 were the checker failing to read the source, or a value that is a correct derivation of its quote.

How the checker went wrong, in order of frequency: the first run happened during a network outage (DNS errors cached as
"missing" or "unfetchable"); IOP and CDS pages return a Radware bot check to scripted fetches; PDF text extraction
splits tables into columns, drops prime signs and ligatures (`fl` becomes `N`, `nu` becomes `l`); arXiv listing abstracts
differ from the PDF's own abstract; HTML copies of a paper add TeX alt text after a formula.

## What was wrong in substance, by stream

Streams follow the owner logs in this directory; the oldest records come from the first research wave and the verification rounds.

**Reactor and solar neutrinos (Homestake, Daya Bay, GALLEX/GNO).**
Homestake was the largest error: the experiment, detector and collaboration were all dated as ending in 1994, on a quote that only gives the span of the 1998 paper's standard data set (1970 to 1994). Davis's Nobel autobiography says the experiment "continued to measure the solar neutrino flux until the late 1990s, when the Homestake Mine ceased operating", and SURF dates the end of the mine to 2001. The end of operation is now an approximate date bounded between 1997 and 2001 in the three records and the placement. The 380 m³ target volume rested on Wikipedia alone and is now sourced to Davis (100,000 gallons) with the 1998 paper's 615 t of tetrachloroethylene. Daya Bay's country set came from Wikipedia and named Chile, which no author affiliation of the final oscillation paper has; the count of six survives but now rests on that author list and depends on counting Taiwan and Hong Kong separately. GALLEX/GNO's six nationalities and "led by MPIK Heidelberg" also rested only on Wikipedia; six holds only as a union over the 1990 GALLEX list and the GNO final paper, and the "led by" claim was removed.

**Colliders-2 (JADE, CELLO, TASSO, MARK J, VEPP-2000, CMD-3).**
The JADE notes were wrong or incomplete in four places: the Frascati meeting was called a "founders meeting" (the paper does not); "no separate approval date is given" (section 3.2 gives October 1976); February 1979 was stated as the date the detector moved into the beam although the same paper's Table 1 and section 3.2 say January 1979; and the end of the MARK J and TASSO construction intervals (1979-01) had no stated basis. For VEPP-2000 the cited copy prints `KMD-3`, not `CMD-3`; the old quote supported neither the 2007 start nor the 2010 end of the interval; the note's "round beams from 2007" contradicts the paper (2008). Quotes now carry the printed text.

**Neutrino-2 (ESSnuSB, ESS).**
ESSnuSB's far-detector technology value said "photomultiplier tubes" on a quote that never mentions them, and its physics goal (CP violation, second oscillation maximum) rested on a sentence about detector baselines; both are re-sourced to the CDR's executive summary. The ESS 5 MW and 2 GeV values were right, but the quote was the arXiv listing's TeX abstract; ESS's own accelerator page now carries them, with a note that they are design requirements of a facility still being commissioned.

**Early waves and DUNE (ProtoDUNE-SP, PS, PSB).**
ProtoDUNE-SP's cosmic-ray tagger was described as "external" and "used together with the beam instrumentation", which the quote (an abstract sentence listing what the paper describes) does not say; it now quotes section 2.6. The PS and PSB injection and beam energy ranges (0.8 to 1.0 GeV) were right, but the 1 GeV bound had no quote of its own.

**Precision measurements and limits (M4).**
The PandaX-4T commissioning-run minimum was plotted and recorded at 30 GeV/c². That figure is in the arXiv listing's abstract; the paper's abstract, body and conclusions all say 40 GeV/c². The marker was 10 GeV/c² off in mass; fixed in the limit, the experiment's display and summary, and the source excerpt.

**Cosmic rays and arrays (KASCADE, LHAASO, H.E.S.S., LST-1, CTAO, KM3NeT, SWGO).**
No value was wrong. Locators were: KASCADE cited a section "Experiment" that does not exist in the paper (seven locators in four files); three LHAASO pool records cited section III.B, which is the WFCTA section, for a sentence in IX.B. The J-PARC beamline check's note called 334 m the extraction-to-dump length, but the sum leaves out the roughly 15 m target station and the dump; it is now a lower bound.

**Low-threshold and CEvNS detectors (COHERENT-Ge, Ricochet).**
COHERENT-Ge's note said five to seven detectors were stable enough; the paper says five of the seven installed, 10.66 kg active, and the 17.6 kg stored value is the nominal array. Ricochet's note said the cited source gave no total for the planned CryoCube; the cited 2025 paper states eighteen 42 g germanium detectors.

**Geometry (Super-Kamiokande cavern).**
The geometry was described as centred on a surveyed "detector centre"; the paper gives only the cavity's position, which the 50 m accuracy already covers.

**Tours.** The Higgs-hunt claim's note said the quoted sentence crosses a page break; it crosses a column break on one page.

**Where nothing was wrong.** Super-Kamiokande inner-detector size (33.8 m by 36.2 m), Kamioka overburden (1000 m of rock, 2700 m.w.e.), the DESI milestone dates (every date in Table 1 of the instrument paper matches; the survey end of 2026-04-15 is the DESI homepage's own statement), ALICE's size and mass, the LST-1/MAGIC spacings, the H.E.S.S. building statements, and the muon-flux values on all ten underground-site records were correct as stored; the checker had failed to read the source, or the value was a derivation that now has a note.
