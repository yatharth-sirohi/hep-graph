How routes are found

One hop between two researchers means they share enough qualifying published works: the works must survive the filters, and the count of shared works must reach the threshold in use. An adviser-to-student record, when the adviser filter is on, is a separate kind of tie read from INSPIRE's own supervision records.

  1. The route is found first. The search reads a stored collaboration map that holds only bare structure — which record, which person, how many authors the record had, what year. It carries no titles and no names. That map is a fixed, labelled version of the source with a cutoff date, not a live reading of INSPIRE, so the same question asked twice against the same version returns the same route.
  2. The details arrive afterwards. Once a route is settled, the paper titles, dates, links, and researcher profiles are fetched for the few people and records actually shown. Those details never steer the search. Nothing read at this stage can add a hop, remove one, or pull the route toward a better-documented person.

What the filters change

Filters do not rank results. They decide which ties exist at all, so each setting produces a different graph to walk.

  • Paper type. Only research literature can carry a coauthorship hop. Theses, books, book chapters, and conference proceedings are set aside.
  • Author-count cap. Small-team papers, large-collaboration papers, or all of them, judged on the full author count printed on the record rather than on the authors resolved here.
  • Minimum shared works. Raising it drops pairs who appear together only once or twice, which removes hops and can lengthen or erase a route.
  • Year range. Works dated outside the range stop counting as evidence, and records with no usable date drop out as soon as any bound is set.
  • Adviser records. Off by default. Turned on, a documented adviser-to-student relation counts as a hop of its own, alongside coauthorship, so a route may pass through a supervision as well as a shared paper.

What a result claims

The engine looks for the shortest route the active filters allow, and says plainly how far it got.

  • Verified route. This route exists and every hop is backed by a documented record. A shorter route has not been ruled out.
  • Proven shortest. No shorter route can exist in the version searched, under the filters in use.
  • Incomplete search. The search stopped early — a limit, a time cutoff, or data that was only partly there. Finding nothing in an incomplete search means nothing.
  • No route. Stated only when coverage for that question is certified complete. Otherwise the same outcome is reported as an incomplete search instead.

What coverage does and does not tell you

Every answer is drawn from one dated version of the source, and that version is partial: records added or corrected upstream since the cutoff are absent, and some records that are present arrive with missing or thin details. So the absence of a route is not proof that no connection exists. Unless a result is marked as complete coverage, silence describes this version of the data, not the researchers.

Connection rules

A coauthor tie requires eligible distinct works, then applies the shared-work threshold. Papers with a full author count above the selected cap are excluded. Thesis, book, book chapter, and proceedings records are excluded.

Adviser ties come from four sources, and every relation says which one it rests on:

  • INSPIRE author profiles - the adviser field on a student's own record.
  • INSPIRE thesis records - the supervisors named on the thesis itself, with its year.
  • Wikidata - doctoral-advisor links where both people carry an identifier that resolves to exactly one INSPIRE record. Names are never matched.
  • Published pages - a small number cited to a primary source such as the researcher's own CV.

Degrees are shown as recorded: PhD where the source says so, and degree not recorded where it does not. A relation marked as a master's, bachelor's or diploma is a different claim and is not shown. Most physicists have no adviser recorded anywhere, so a missing adviser usually means none was recorded, not that none existed.

Identity & evidence

People use stable profile IDs. Author names are never merged as an identity rule. Each path edge lists documented source works that support it.

Some people appear only as names on paper bylines, with no INSPIRE author profile of their own. They are marked as having no profile, and nothing is claimed about records they are missing.

Dates & counts

Year filtering uses the earliest documented preprint or publication date. Records with unknown dates remain only when no date bound is selected. Author caps use the original full author list, not just resolved authors.

Adviser relations are dated by their thesis year. Only thesis records carry one, so about half of all relations are dated, and most of those are from the last fifteen years.

The family map

The Family map draws every documented adviser-to-student relation at once: each box is a researcher, advisers sit above their students, and separate groups are drawn side by side. Select anyone to see their advisers, students, how far their recorded lineage reaches in each direction, and their thesis years.

  • How two people are related. Pick two people and the map names the relationship the way a family tree would - adviser, academic siblings, second cousins once removed - by finding the nearest ancestor they share. Where they share none, it traces the chain of supervision that joins them instead, usually through someone who studied under two advisers.
  • Everyone between them. A result can also show every person on any of the shortest chains, not just one route.
  • Distance from a chosen person. Set anyone as a reference point and every other selection shows how many supervision links separate them.
  • By thesis year. A timeline plays the recorded relations in order of thesis year. Undated relations are hidden unless you ask for them.

Every answer describes the records, not the people: no recorded chain between two physicists means none is documented here.
